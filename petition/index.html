✊ A charter for live music

Music money goes to the venues and artists first.

Not to the ticketing giants. Not to the streaming platforms. To the people who make the music and the rooms that host it. Let's make it the standard.

Why this matters to me

I'm 28. I got in trouble as a kid, mostly out of boredom. Nothing on, nowhere to be. Music is what pulled me out.

A pub with a gig on is not a luxury. It's safety infrastructure. A room with music has eyes on the street, someone's always awake, and active streets keep trouble away. Nightlife is the release valve that lets the working week stay sane, and the community it grows looks after its own.

But survival is economic. Costs to run events keep climbing, and the fees big tech charges bleed these rooms dry. If we lose nightlife, we lose the youth. We lose the one place that gives a bored kid somewhere to go instead of the streets.

So I built free, open tools for pubs and musicians, and I gave them away. Because the cost is the thing killing the rooms. I want people to live better than I did.

From the youth that has seen a lot in his days.

What's broken

Ticket giants can take as much as 20 to 30%+ in booking fees on every ticket to a local gig.
Streaming pays artists fractions of a cent per play. You need a million streams to make rent.
Grassroots venues pay monthly rent to big platforms just to have a website and sell a ticket.
The people who make and host the music are the last to get paid, not the first.

The charter — make this the standard

  1. Artists and venues get paid first. The money from a gig goes to the people who made it and hosted it, before any platform takes a cut.
  2. No compulsory middleman fees on grassroots live music. If a venue wants to sell a ticket, it shouldn't have to hand a giant a slice to do it.
  3. Free, open tools for everyone. Websites, ticketing, sign-ups and sales that any venue or artist can run themselves, at no cost, owning their own data.
  4. Public money for live music backs venues and artists directly — especially in the areas that have no live music at all.
We, the undersigned, call on venues, councils, arts bodies and government to adopt this charter as the standard for live music: that the money, the tools and the support go to the artists and venues first. We back free and open infrastructure so that no grassroots room is priced out of existence, and no community loses its nightlife to fees it was never meant to pay.
