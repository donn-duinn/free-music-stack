House of Donn

Why this exists

I'm 28. I got in trouble as a kid, mostly out of boredom. Nothing on, nowhere to be. Music is what pulled me out. So I want people to live better than I did.

A pub with a gig on is not a luxury. It is safety infrastructure.

A room with music has eyes on the street. Someone is always awake. The strip stays lit, and lit streets keep trouble away.

Nightlife is a living organism. It is the release valve that lets the nine to five switch off and stay sane, the thing that makes Monday survivable. And the community it grows looks after its own. Regulars, staff and artists watch each other's backs. The scene protects itself, for as long as it can survive.

I have gotten more real advice standing in a pub than I ever got at the dining room table. Blue collar boys grow up the same. We build the houses on minimum wage, and we need somewhere to put the day down and have a beer. And the same room holds the ones who want to sing, to dance, to do their makeup and take the stage. One room, everyone in it, everyone allowed to be exactly who they are.

Let the working man have his beer, and let the artist have the stage. Same room. That is the point.

That is the catch. Survival is economic. Costs to run events keep climbing, and the fees big tech charges bleed these rooms dry. If we lose nightlife, we lose the youth. We lose the one place that gives a bored kid somewhere to go instead of the streets.

I am writing this with nothing to my name. In debt, hurt at work, and priced out of the very thing this is about. The pub, the music, that was my last escape, the last place I could let my hair down, and the cost of living took it from me. I got priced out of culture.

I am not writing this from the outside looking in. I am writing it from inside the problem. I know exactly what it costs to lose this, because I am losing it.

So the tools are free. All of them. Given away, not sold. Because the cost is the thing killing the rooms, and the fastest way to save them is to take the cost off the table.

And I'll say this plainly: I am going to get things wrong. I already have. I'm not doing this because I'm the most skilled person for the job. I'm doing it because I noticed a gap, I've got enough to make a start, and someone had to stand up. That's why I'm reaching out so wide, not to show off, but because I need help. If you know more than me, if you can build a piece I can't, come and help me make it work. This was never about one person being good enough. It's about enough people giving a damn.

This is for all walks of life. Everyone deserves a room where they can put the mask down and be exactly who they are. The tradie and the drag queen, the shift worker and the songwriter, the kid who's lost and the kid who's found. Same room. All of us.

Put music where there is none, and you make a place safer, kinder, and more alive. That is worth more to me than money.

From the youth that has seen a lot in his days.
