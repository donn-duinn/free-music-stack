A doctrine

Gonzo Engineering

by Daniel Hogben (donn) · Tech Duinn
Gonzo Engineering — building from inside the problem, in the open, at full speed: you don't pitch the idea, you ship it live and hand people the proof. First person, fearless, fast, and given away. The demo is the argument. The product is the proof.

Gonzo journalism put the writer inside the story instead of pretending to watch from outside. Gonzo Engineering does the same with building. You're not a consultant with a deck. You're the person the problem happened to, building your way out of it in public, and letting the running thing speak for itself.

They raise money to promise things. I build the things and give them away.

The rules

  1. Proof over promises.Don't pitch it, ship it. A live thing you can click beats any slide. Show it running.
  2. Build from inside the problem.The best person to fix a gap is the one it's hurting. Feel it, then build it.
  3. Speed is honesty.Move before you talk yourself out of it. 48 hours beats six months of meetings. Momentum is the moat.
  4. Fail out loud.I'll get things wrong, and I say so. Rejected 100 times, the 101st is the one. Mistakes in the open are how you get help.
  5. Give it away.The code isn't the moat. Who you are is. You can't be out-priced by free, and you can't be copied when the thing being copied is you.
  6. One starts it, a movement finishes it.One person can prove it's possible. It takes a crew to make it last. So build the proof, then ask for help.

That's not recklessness. It's the opposite. It's refusing to hide behind process, funding rounds and permission, and instead putting a real, working, honest thing in front of people and saying: here, it exists, use it.

— the youth that has seen a lot in his days
