🦺 A fair go for the crew

Crew First

The people who build the show, paid what they're worth.

"I gave seven years and my body to holding shows up. When I got hurt, I got dropped. The people who do the work should not be the last ones paid and the first ones forgotten."

What's broken

Event crew — stagehands, riggers, AV, audio, lighting — are the ones who actually build every gig, festival and show. But the labour-hire model takes a fat margin between the client and the worker, keeps everyone casual, and walks away the moment someone breaks.

It runs on fast turnover and pressure — heavy hours, aggressive push to keep bodies working, churn through people and discard them. The margin is skimmed off the very people carrying the weight, and the work itself destroys them. That's not a business model. That's grinding people down for parts.

The fix

Most of what the agency charges that margin for is coordination — matching crew to gigs, scheduling, the admin. AI can do that. So AI does the booking work, and the margin it was hiding goes back to the crew. The people who build the show get booked more directly and paid what they're actually worth.

How it works

1
AI does the matching.Clients post the gig, crew get matched by skill, ticket and availability — the admin an agency skims a margin for, automated and free.
2
The crew keep the margin.Transparent rates. You see exactly what the gig pays and what you take. No hidden cut between you and the client.
3
Protections done properly.Super, tax, workers comp, public liability, WHS inductions — kept and done right, co-op style. Fair pay never means fewer protections.
4
Never dropped when you're hurt.Built by someone who was. Care and hardship support baked in, not an afterthought.
