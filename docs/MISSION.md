# 🔥 THE MISSION — free, open, local. Against big tech.

**openmic + the venue suite = the tools big tech charges rent for, given away to grassroots
music and local artists. Free. Open-source. Local-first. No rent, no data grab, no middleman.**

## The enemy (what we're against)
Big tech extracts rent from the smallest players who can least afford it:
- **Website builders** (Squarespace/Wix) — $30+/mo for a small bar to have a page.
- **POS + loyalty SaaS** (Square/Toast) — % of every sale + monthly fees + your customer data.
- **Social platforms** (Meta) — gatekeep who sees a local gig or a local artist, then charge to reach them.
- **Marketplaces** (Etsy/Ticketmaster) — take a cut of every sale from artists who keep almost nothing.
- **Data** — all of it harvested, sold, locked in.

## The answer (what we build)
A free, open-licence toolkit any Melbourne venue can run for $0, that keeps money and data with
the people who make the culture:
1. **openmic** — free open-mic / emerging-artist sign-up (vs chaos / paid tools). LIVE.
2. **Free venue websites** — one page: gig nights, menus, contact (vs $30/mo builders). BUILT (template).
3. **Menus** — drinks + food, editable, free (vs menu SaaS).
4. **Local Art Wall** — the art on the venue's walls, sold **direct to the artist**, no cut taken (vs Etsy).
5. **Loyalty** — a simple, free digital loyalty/stamp system on the venue's own data (vs Square/Toast rent). [BUILD NEXT]
6. **Blue-team / security help** — protect small venues (wifi, POS, backups, phishing) and keep their
   data local + theirs (vs surveillance capitalism). [OFFER]
7. **The Newsletter** — a free "what's on" listing for Melbourne open mics & local music (vs paywalled guides).

## Why we win (the unfair advantage)
Big tech *must* extract rent to exist. **We don't** — it runs on free Cloudflare tiers, open-source, and
one musician who ships. They can't compete with free-and-open without killing their own business model.
Every venue that adopts this is money and data that *stops* flowing to big tech and stays local.

## The through-line
Local-first primitives. Open licence. Money stays with artists + venues. Community-owned, not platform-owned.
Built from inside the problem by a self-taught musician who just wants it to exist everywhere.

## Why it's fundable / press-worthy
This is the story: *a self-taught, injured Melbourne musician is building free, open-source infrastructure
to take grassroots music and local venues back from big tech — and councils are already adopting it.*
That wins grants (civic tech + arts), gets press (David vs Goliath, local hero), and gets venues on board
(free stuff that helps them + fights the platforms squeezing them).

## Roadmap (wedge → suite)
1. **Wedge:** openmic + free venue site (get venues saying yes — done, live).
2. **Stack value:** menus + art wall (built) → loyalty (next) → security offer.
3. **Spread:** the newsletter + press + council adoption → it becomes the local default.
4. **Sustain:** grants fund the build; venues + artists never pay; big tech loses ground.
