Priced out of live music, a Preston musician built an anti-big-tech platform with AI in 48 hours — then gave it away free

A broke, injured musician has built a free, open-source alternative to Ticketek, Spotify, Bandcamp and Squarespace in 48 hours, and released the whole thing for nothing, as proof that AI can be used to pay artists rather than replace them.

Daniel Hogben, 28, started with an idea on Tuesday night. Forty-eight hours later the "Free Music Stack" was live: a complete toolkit that gives grassroots venues and independent artists the digital infrastructure big tech charges rent for, with every dollar routed to the artist or the venue.

It includes fee-free ticketing (no Ticketek cut), tools to sell music and merch direct while keeping 100 percent, free venue and artist websites, open-mic sign-up, a what's-on calendar, and a mental-health help page with Australian crisis lines built in. All of it is open source, MIT licensed, and free forever.

"I got priced out of the one thing that kept me going. Live music, the pub, the open mic, that was my last escape, and the cost of living took it. I built this from inside the problem, not looking in at it."

Hogben, who is recovering from a workplace injury and facing housing insecurity, built the platform using AI, deliberately, to counter the narrative that AI is coming for musicians' livelihoods.

"Artists are told to fear AI. I used it to build the opposite, a thing that pays them. It went from an idea to a live platform in 48 hours. That's the proof: AI can be used to help the arts."

Within days, a local council, a state music body and community radio stations have been in contact, and a public charter, calling for the money from live music to go to venues and artists first, is collecting signatures. Hogben charges for none of it.

"My role is to keep it free and keep building it, so artists get the space to do what they do best: make people happy. If it grows, it's so we can hire people to keep it free for everyone."
